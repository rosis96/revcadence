Decide whether a company is a good fit for RevCadence using only what you can observe on their website. Never assume revenue, company size, customer lifetime value, deal size, sales maturity, funding, or how complex their sales cycle is — if it isn't stated, treat it as unknown, never as a reason to reject.

How to reason, in order:
1. First work out what the company actually SELLS — their primary business model — before anything else.
2. Then work out WHO buys it: other businesses (B2B) or consumers (B2C).
3. Then work out HOW it's sold: is the offering consultative, project-based, managed, strategic, implementation-based, or relationship-driven (i.e. it needs conversations, proposals, consultations, or custom engagements)?
4. Only after you understand the business model should you look at the industries they serve. The industries named on a site almost always describe their customers, case studies, or target markets — they do NOT define the company's own business model. Never accept or reject a company because of the industries it serves.
5. Never classify on keywords alone. "Ecommerce", "healthcare", "education", "manufacturing" on a page usually describe who they serve, not what they are — check which it is before deciding.

Return ICP when: the company clearly provides B2B services or complex B2B solutions that typically require conversations, proposals, consultations, or custom engagements, AND you can see at least two supporting signals, AND nothing on the site is a hard disqualifier.

Good signals (a fit): B2B service providers and professional services of any kind — consulting (business, management, technology, IT, digital transformation, AI), agencies (marketing, advertising, branding, creative, PR, digital, recruitment), software development / custom software, engineering and industrial services, managed service providers, financial advisory, accounting, legal, HR consulting, training, research, video/event production, B2B media, architecture, commercial construction / interiors / real estate advisory, environmental or healthcare consulting, business process outsourcing. Site cues that support a fit: "Request a proposal", "Book a consultation", "Contact sales", "Request a demo", "Book a discovery call", case studies, portfolio, client success stories, tailored/enterprise/business solutions, custom or enterprise pricing, "request a quote".

Return Needs Review when: the company looks relevant but the evidence is incomplete or you can't confidently identify the business model. When in doubt, Needs Review — do not reject. This is the default.

Return Non-ICP only when the company's primary business model clearly falls outside our market and you can point to a real disqualifier. Clear disqualifiers: consumer ecommerce / DTC / physical-product brands, retail stores, marketplaces, dropshipping, restaurants and local consumer services (HVAC, roofing, landscaping, cleaning, salon, spa, dentist, gym, auto repair, pet grooming, local clinics, local retail), consumer healthcare or consumer education providers, schools / colleges / universities, government agencies / departments / municipalities / public sector, military, nonprofits / charities / foundations, religious organizations, personal brands / influencers / content creators / affiliate sites, news sites, non-operational sites, and pure self-service SaaS with standardized pricing and no sales interaction.

Important nuances:
- SaaS is fine if it's sales-led or enterprise — implementation services, custom onboarding, consulting included, enterprise pricing, or "request a demo". Only reject SaaS that is purely self-service (instant signup, standard monthly pricing, no sales interaction).
- Distinguish an ecommerce COMPANY (an online retailer / DTC brand → Non-ICP) from a company that SELLS SERVICES TO ecommerce businesses (an agency/consultancy serving ecommerce → ICP).
- "Commercial" and "industrial" versions of local trades are a fit (commercial construction, commercial HVAC, industrial maintenance, commercial security, commercial facility management); the consumer/local versions are not.
- Pricing alone never decides it. Custom/enterprise/"contact sales" pricing supports a fit; self-service checkout with instant purchase and no consultation points away.
- Avoid companies whose entire addressable market is extremely narrow (e.g. serving only nuclear plants, only uranium miners, only airport baggage systems, only Ivy League universities). A normal industry focus (healthcare, ecommerce, manufacturing, logistics, financial firms) is fine and expected.

When you return Non-ICP, base it on a positive fact that matches a disqualifier — never on missing information. If the only reason would be "the site doesn't say," return Needs Review instead.
